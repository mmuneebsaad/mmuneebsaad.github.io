🎰 Asset or gambling token? What leading economists actually say about Bitcoin.

Sep 2026 Dr Muhammad Muneeb Saad 4 min read

As researchers, we are trained to follow the evidence and the expert literature. On cryptocurrency, the verdict from many of the world's most credentialed economists and investors is strikingly critical.

  1. Warren Buffett (2023): "Bitcoin is a gambling token, and it doesn't have any intrinsic value" — it generates no earnings, dividends, or cash flow; its price depends solely on the next buyer.
  2. Paul Krugman, Nobel Laureate: After 15+ years, Bitcoin "remains economically useless," with illicit finance among its dominant applications.
  3. Nouriel Roubini (NYU, 2018 US Senate testimony): Bitcoin fails all three classical functions of money — unit of account, means of payment, and store of value — calling much of crypto "a Ponzi game. A scam."
  4. Eugene Fama, Nobel Laureate: Assigns Bitcoin's long-term collapse a probability "close to 100%," citing lack of intrinsic value and reliance on belief rather than fundamentals.
  5. Steve Hanke (Johns Hopkins): Bitcoin has zero fundamental value — no cash flows, no state backing, purely speculative pricing.
  6. European Central Bank: Bitcoin lacks intrinsic value and fails the Misesian Regression Theorem of money.
🔬 The scientific takeaway

Unlike productive assets, Bitcoin has no physical backing, generates no output, and its valuation rests on sentiment alone — properties economists associate with speculative bubbles and gambling, not currency. Proponents dispute this framing, but retail investors should understand that the downside case is endorsed by Nobel laureates and central banks — and most late buyers in past cycles suffered heavy losses.

⚠️ Invest in knowledge before you invest in tokens.
